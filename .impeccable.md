## Design Context

### Users

English-speaking learners exploring ancient Greek music and language, including
musicians who already know modern mode names. The user requested specific,
source-grounded discoveries, operable visual explanations and substantial learning
value rather than generic showcase content.

### Brand Personality

Scholarly, curious, tangible. Let a learner hear a claim, inspect its mechanism and
read the evidence without requiring prior Greek.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve the existing silver-page and purple-codex themes, Gentium Book Plus and
GFS Neohellenic. Use the manuscript's text and a purposeful interval drawing as
the visual focus. Avoid decorative dashboards, generic cards and gamification.

### Design Principles

1. Change one musical variable at a time; label modern teaching choices.
2. Keep Greek, literal English and the printed page within the same investigation.
3. Give sound a readable equivalent and make controls work by keyboard.
4. Preserve useful work as a portable reading with its actual evidence and audio.
5. Retain the existing visual language; use spacious comparisons and compact tables.
